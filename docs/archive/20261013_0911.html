Phantom Zernike Wavefront Sensor: Phase Contrast with Engineered Focal Plane Reference Speckles
Authors: Benjamin Courtney-Barrer
First: 2026-10-08T23:30:00+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-08T23:30:00+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
We introduce the Phantom Zernike Wavefront Sensor (phantom ZWFS), an engineered reference extension of phase-contrast wavefront sensing in which controlled pupil plane modulation generates off-axis coherent speckle structures that undergo localized focal plane phase shifts. These references may replace the central ZWFS reference when the on-axis PSF core is required for another optical function, such as photonic science coupling, or may augment a conventional central ZWFS response to provide additional wavefront information. Using a monochromatic scalar model, we derive the resulting carrier structure and show how the engineered references can be recovered through spatial demodulation and incorporated into calibrated reconstruction. A simple four-reference realization demonstrates that the reference geometry redistributes photon sensitivity across spatial frequency magnitude and orientation, producing mode dependent changes in linearity and cross-talk rather than a uniform improvement over a classical ZWFS. We then consider differential piston on a seven segment aperture as an application specific example. A hybrid sensor retaining the central ZWFS phase region and adding six engineered references that match the segment geometry gives similar precision near the phased state but substantially improved performance at larger piston excursions. Under the adopted photon-noise-limited benchmark, the hybrid reduces the nonlinear reconstruction error and provides better conditioned optical encoding of the piston state than an idealized vector ZWFS, allowing more precise piston estimation from the same detected photon flux at larger piston excursions.

Vectorial Control of Scintillation in Kolmogorov Turbulence and Quality Parametrization
Authors: Shouvik Sadhukhan
First: 2026-03-07T06:33:21+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-06T18:20:00+00:00
Comments: 17 pages, 13 figures
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Intensity scintillation limits free-space optical links in strong turbulence. We examine experimentally how sequential linear polarization filtering modifies the scintillation of a laser beam distorted by Kolmogorov-type turbulence, and interpret the results with a vector-field and fourth-order-moment model. A collimated He--Ne beam (632.8~nm) crossed a rotating pseudo-random phase plate (Fried parameter $r_0\approx0.6$~mm) and zero to five co-aligned thin-film polarizers; 200 camera frames were recorded per configuration, together with a turbulence-free reference. For fully developed speckle the model gives a total-field index $σ_I^2=(1+P^2)/2$ in terms of the degree of polarization $P$. From zero to five polarizers, the Gaussian-envelope scintillation index fell from 0.083 to 0.002 and the single-pixel index from 0.80 to 0.27 (about 66\%), whereas beam wander remained comparable across configurations. As Gaussian statistics alone cannot explain this reduction, we attribute it to rejection of a depolarized, strongly fluctuating component and derive a quantitative criterion for this mechanism. Passive polarization filtering thus offers a simple, low-cost route to scintillation mitigation in free-space optical terminals.

A Multimodal Hyperspectral and Laser Speckle Imaging Platform for Simultaneous Monitoring of Spinal Cord Oxygenation, Perfusion and Metabolism
Authors: Junda Wang, Luca Giannoni, Ayse Gertrude Yenicelik, Eleni Giama, Frederic Lange, Ilias Tachtsidis
First: 2026-10-06T08:25:08+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-06T08:25:08+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Hyperspectral imaging (HSI) enables spatial mapping of haemoglobin oxygenation and cytochrome-c-oxidase (CCO) redox changes in exposed neural tissue. In this study, we present a technical upgrade of an existing HSI platform by the integration of laser speckle contrast imaging (LSCI), enabling contemporaneous assessment of oxygenation, oxidative metabolism, relative perfusion and a derived relative oxygen metabolic index in exposed spinal cord in a rat preclinical model. The upgraded system (hNIR+) uses a shared optical path and a single camera to acquire HSI and LSCI images from the same field of view using a sequential acquisition workflow. Hyperspectral images are acquired at 11 discrete wavelengths (600, 630, 665, 784, 800, 818, 835, 851, 868, 881, and 894 nm) to estimate changes in oxygenated haemoglobin (HbO2), deoxygenated haemoglobin (HHb) and oxidized CCO (oxCCO). LSCI is implemented using 632.8-nm coherent illumination to generate relative blood-flow index (rBFI) maps. The LSCI subsystem was validated using a controlled blood-flow phantom, where the LSCI-derived inverse speckle contrast index showed a strong positive linear relationship with pump-controlled flow. The upgraded platform was then evaluated in proof-of-concept observations of the rat spinal cord under normoxia, hypoxia and recovery conditions. Representative multimodal maps and ROI-based time-course analysis showed clear dynamic responses in HbO2, HHb, oxCCO and rBFI during oxygen challenges that were consistent with the expected physiological responses of the spinal cord during hypoxia, hyperoxia and subsequent recoveries. Overall, these results demonstrate that integrating LSCI into HSI extends the platform to multimodal and simultaneous monitoring of haemodynamic-metabolic coupling.
